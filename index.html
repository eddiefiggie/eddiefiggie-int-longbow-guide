Start here: what this guide is

This is a complete plan for one Dungeons & Dragons Online character: an Intelligence-based archer with a longbow, from level 1 to the level cap of 36 and into Reaper 10, the hardest difficulty in the game. Every number comes from the DDO wiki page named in Sources. Where a choice is a judgment call rather than a wiki fact, it is labelled build decision.

It is a platform build. DDO rewards playing a character to cap, reincarnating, and levelling again for a permanent past-life bonus. This build is the platform you do that on: the same Rogue 1 / Ranger 9 chassis every life with ten levels of whichever class you are collecting, the same enhancement and destiny layouts, and gear picked up while levelling that stays useful on the next life. One character, every grind, no re-planning.

What you will do with it. Shoot from range with Intelligence driving both your attack and your damage (Harper Agent’s Strategic Combat does that), mark a target so the whole party hits it harder, and press a small set of buttons on short timers. Playing it, at the end, explains every button and every defence, gives a rotation that works at Reaper 10, and says what kills this character and what to do about it.

Take the first-time Reaper bonuses on every life. Reaper difficulty gives Reaper experience (RXP) on top of normal XP. The wiki’s Reaper difficulty page records that the first run of a quest on any Reaper skull in the current life pays an extra 200% RXP, with a +45% first-time XP bonus on the quest itself. RXP buys Reaper enhancement points that persist through reincarnation (re-allocate them at level 4), and it is only awarded when at least ten enemies are defeated. Run each quest once on Reaper 1 as you level; the bonus resets with every new life, so a platform build collects it again and again.

Why ten levels of the past-life class. Past Life Feats page: the past life goes to “the class you have the most levels of. In the case of a tie, the dominant class is the first listed on this list: Barbarian, Bard, Cleric, Fighter, Paladin, Ranger, Rogue, Sorcerer, Wizard, Monk, Favored Soul, Artificer, Druid, Warlock, Alchemist.” Ranger is sixth, so a 9/9 tie with any later class hands the life to Ranger. Ten levels of the past-life class win outright; one Rogue level can never be dominant.

Why Rogue at level 1. Rogue page: only “Arcane Tricksters, Rogues, Artificers, Dark Hunter Ranger archetype or those under the effect of the spell Find Traps” can Search for traps with DC above 20. Rogue 1 also gives a ranged sneak die (“can be used with ranged weapons within 30 ft.”), (8 + INT) x4 skill points, and Disable Device, Open Lock, Search, Spot, UMD and Tumble as class skills; Rogue is a free class. If you never trap, Fighter 1 is the alternative: a bonus feat from a list that includes Point Blank Shot, Precision, Weapon Focus, Power Critical and Improved Precise Shot, and full BAB that brings Improved Precise Shot to level 12 on every life.

Iconic lives. Iconic True Reincarnation “rewards both the Iconic PL and full Class PL benefits.” Level 1 is the iconic’s class, so Rogue moves to 2 and the base class fills 12-20. The Aasimar Scourge’s forced class is Ranger (“Scourge first level is Ranger”), so that life reaches Ranger 11 and Improved Precise Shot is free at level 12.

Racial lives. Racial Reincarnation grants “a Racial Past Life feat, based on the character’s current race”; class is irrelevant, so the split is chosen for power: Ranger 9 for Manyshot and Evasion, Rogue 11 for Evasion at level 2, 6d6 sneak attack and the Rogue 10 special ability.

Class grants used. Ranger (prerequisites waived): Bow Strength 1, Rapid Shot 2, Precise Shot 4, Manyshot 6, Evasion 9; Favored Enemy at 1 and 5; Improved Precise Shot at 11. Rogue: Sneak Attack +1d6 and Trapfinding 1, Evasion 2, +1d6 at every odd level, Improved Uncanny Dodge 8, Special Ability 10, 6d6 at 11.

The journey: 45 trips, 89 past lives

What a trip is. One climb of the character to level 30 followed by reincarnation. It is the unit of effort in this plan, and it comes in two kinds:

Trip typeClimbHearts usedPast lives banked
Iconic tripStart at 15 as an iconic, climb to 30Iconic True Heart of Wood at 30Two: the iconic feat and the class the iconic carried (an Aasimar Scourge trip is one Ranger stack and one Scourge stack)
Heroic-race tripStart at 1 as a heroic race, climb to 30Epic Heart of Wood at 30, which puts you back at 20; then a Heroic True Heart (class life) or a Heroic True Heart of Blood (racial life) at 20Two: one epic life and one class life, or one epic life and one racial life

A trip never pays two stacks of the same feat. The full bonus of anything is three separate lives, each on its own trip: three Ranger stacks are three Scourge trips; twelve Divine epic lives are twelve heroic-race trips that each used an Epic Heart. That is why 45 trips bank 89 past lives, and why the “stacks” in the table count up one at a time.

Worked example, trip 4. After three Scourge trips, reincarnate into a heroic race and run Dark Hunter 1 to 30. At 30, use an Epic Heart and choose Primal: Doubleshot (epic life 1 of 3). Re-level to 20 in the place between, use a Heroic True Heart, and because Dark Hunter is the dominant class the life pays Dark Hunter (stack 1 of 3). Trips 5 and 6 repeat it; then trip 7 starts at 15 as a Shadar-kai.

Why the plan looks like this. Two rules. First, every trip to 30 pays two past lives, so nothing is run for one: the class lives that must be heroic (Dark Hunter x3, Dragon Disciple x3, Artificer, Druid, Favored Soul, Alchemist) and the racial lives worth having (Drow x3, Gnome x3, Dwarf x2) are eighteen heroic-race trips, exactly the eighteen epic lives the build wants, so the epic lives ride the heroic trips and no iconic trip ever needs the extra 20-to-30 climb. Second, each bonus is finished to three stacks before the next one starts, in order of value to the bow, so the character is never carrying a half-built line. The one block that is not a triple is Completionist: seven one-off lives that pay a single feat (+2 all abilities, +2 all skills). Its placement after the Tier A triples and before Tier B remains a build decision. For the selected INT-to-damage route, its +1 INT modifier contributes +1 to hit and +2 ability-based bow damage once both Precise Shot and Improved Precise Shot are acquired; their separate +50% passive contributions stack. Know the Angles supplies its own conditional Insight bonus and is not doubled by those feats. Precise Shot · Improved Precise Shot · Harper Agent.

Skills

Spot, Search, Disable Device, Open Lock, Auto-Search, Use Magic Device and Augment pages read 30 October 2026.

Skills are where the Rogue level pays for itself. Two wiki rules shape the whole plan. First, one level of a class “permanently raises that skill’s maximum rank to (3 + character level)” for every one of that class’s skills (Cross-class skill page), so Rogue at level 1 lets Disable Device, Open Lock, Use Magic Device, Tumble, Bluff, Diplomacy and Balance be trained to the full maximum (23 at level 20) for the rest of the life. Second, each rank costs 1 skill point when the skill is a class skill of the class being advanced, or 2 points when it is cross-class and can be trained (Skill point and Class skill). A prior Rogue level raises those maximums; it does not make later levels use Rogue prices. Ranger’s class skills are Concentration, Heal, Hide, Jump, Listen, Move Silently, Search, Spot and Swim (Ranger page), so those seven Rogue skills cost 2 per rank on Ranger levels. Past-life-class levels use the class being advanced, which can make some of those skills 1-point purchases.

The cost column gives named class-skill examples, not an exhaustive list of classes or archetypes. Check the actual class being advanced, including its archetype; an unlisted class is not automatically cross-class. The 8 November source notes retain the unresolved Barbarian Balance and Dark Hunter Spot pricing limits. These price corrections do not verify the existing total budgets or change the selected ranks and priorities.

Training eligibility, maximum ranks and price are separate. Disable Device and Open Lock can be trained only when at least one class already taken has that skill as a class skill. Ordinary Ranger 1 cannot train either; paying a cross-class price does not make it eligible. UMD and Tumble can be trained cross-class. During heroic training (levels 1–20), a skill that is cross-class for all classes taken has a maximum of (3 + character level) / 2 ranks; taking a class that has it raises the maximum to character level + 3. Rogue-first class and racial lives can train Disable Device and Open Lock at character level 1, with a maximum of 4 ranks. On Scourge, Rogue 1 is taken at character level 2: the Rogue class-skill ceiling is 5 then, 6 at level 3 and 23 at level 20. These are ceilings, not purchased ranks; each purchase still uses the class being advanced. Choosing iconic levels 1–15 in one sitting does not let a later Rogue level supply eligibility at the earlier Ranger-1 training step. Dated source-ledger evidence and limits.

The budget. Level 1 gives four times the normal points (Skills page): Rogue’s (8 + Intelligence modifier) × 4 is 52 at the build’s Intelligence 20. Ranger levels give 6 + modifier (11-13 each as the tomes land, about 109 over levels 2-10), and the past-life class gives its own base + modifier (Fighter 2, Monk 4, Rogue 8). Only the base score and the Intelligence tome count toward points; items and buffs do not. A Fighter life has about 266 points to level 20; a Rogue life far more. Epic levels add ranks on top (the UMD page counts up to 10 of them).

Spotting and Auto-Search (wiki). Two different things find traps for you, and the build has only one of them before level 23. Spot is passive and has no roll: “if your score is high enough you will always succeed and if it’s too low you’ll never know about it” (Spot page), and the same threshold rule gives the “you sense a secret door” message. That is why Spot is now at maximum: under the DC, nothing fires. A spotted trap still needs a manual Search to find the box. Auto-Search is the ability that searches for you: “a Spot check against the DC of the findable object, which is successful is then immediately followed by a Search check of the same DC” (Auto-Search page), so it needs both skills high. Its sources are the elf-family racial line Nothing Is Hidden (Eladrin and Chaosmancer tier 1, Drow, Elf and Morninglord tier 2, Shadar-kai tier 4, with a -12/-8/-4 penalty that Keen Senses offsets), the Inquisitive core Mind Like Iron (-4), and Watchful Eye, the epic feat at 27 that Shiradi’s Pierce Deception grants at tier 3, with no penalty but “cannot detect secret doors at all” since U81. So: traps find themselves from level 23 on every life; on elf-family and Shadar-kai lives the racial AP buys Nothing Is Hidden and secret doors find themselves too; on every other life before 23, Spot warns and you press Search.

Before changing a Lamordia augment

Use this checklist when filling the heroic Calamitous or changing the crafted endgame bow. The retained crafting page (revision 720728) distinguishes slot compatibility, item level and recovery:

  1. Match the full slot type. Compare the augment tooltip’s Augment Type with the item’s slot. The family and variant must match: Melancholic (Weapon) fits Melancholic Slot (Weapon), not Melancholic Slot (Armor). Use Weapon augments for the bow’s Lamordia weapon slots.
  2. Keep a levelling bow usable. Heroic slots accept Legendary augments and vice versa, but a Legendary augment raises a heroic item’s minimum level to 34. For the heroic Calamitous route, choose the heroic versions in the recipe above; fitting the slot alone does not make an augment suitable for your level.
  3. Recover before replacing if you want to keep the old augment. Placing a new augment in an occupied slot can destroy the slotted augment. A Viktranium Pick removes a Lamordia augment and returns it unharmed for use elsewhere; a Jeweler’s Tool Kit does not work. Check that you have the Pick before changing a fill you intend to keep.

What this character is, in plain words

This is a glass archer. Its whole design is that it does not get hit: it kills from range, it makes other people the target, and when something does reach it, it leaves. It is not a character that stands in a doorway and trades blows. If you play it like a melee that happens to hold a bow, it dies, and it dies fast, because almost nothing in the build is spent on taking damage.

Three constraints set the tone. First, hit points: Ranger rolls a d10 per level and Rogue a d8 (class pages), and the stats put everything into Intelligence and the Dexterity the feats need, so Constitution is whatever is left. Second, healing: do not plan around reliable in-combat self-healing on high Reaper. Starting Wisdom 8 is not the final equipped score; check Ranger spell access against actual Ranger level, total equipped Wisdom and prepared spells. The new Wisdom gear is not proof of usable or sufficient healing, and on Reaper self-healing is reduced (40% at one skull, 4% at ten: Reaper difficulty page). Third, armor: light armor only, which is what keeps Evasion working (“wearing medium or heavy armor” negates it: Evasion page) and what caps Magical Resistance Rating at 100 (MRR page). The build decision remains to prioritize avoidance, position and a party healer. No spell, feat or healing rotation is newly selected here.

What it is good at: single targets and packs both, from the first second of a fight, with debuffs that make the whole party hit harder. The Mark cuts a target’s fortification, AC, saves and speed for everyone. Harmonic Resonance, Track and Shadow Loss are party-wide debuffs too. That is why a bow earns a Reaper slot where its own damage is cut to 10%: the debuffs are not cut.

Your abilities, explained

The table in the next part gives every cooldown; this part says what each ability is for. They are grouped by job.

Standing buffs: things you switch on once.

  • Precision (feat stance). Always on. It is your fortification bypass: -25% to the target’s fortification so your criticals and sneak dice land. There is no reason to ever turn it off.
  • Archer’s Focus (the stance that comes with Precise Shot). Always on. It is the single biggest damage line in the build and it is the reason the build stands still, which the section on standing still explains in full. The stance itself does nothing until you have stood still for 3 seconds; after that every hit adds 5 Ranged Power, to a cap of 25 stacks with Improved Archer’s Focus. That is +125 Ranged Power, more than every item you own put together. (Wiki: Archer’s Focus.)
  • Improved Precise Shot (feat stance, from level 12). Two things in one feat. The passive half (+50% of your Intelligence modifier as damage) is always on whether the stance is lit or not. The stance half makes every arrow pass through every enemy in a line at -20% damage. Light it for corridors and packs, put it out for a boss: against one target it is a straight 20% loss.
  • Haste (Horizon Walker core, a spell-like ability). Recast every two minutes. +15% attack speed. It is an enhancement bonus, so the Ranged Alacrity on your quiver does not add to it, but it does stack with Action Boosts.
  • Know the Angles (Harper trance). Recast every two minutes. While active, it supplies a separate Insight bonus to damage equal to half your Intelligence modifier. If actual equipped and buffed Intelligence is in the 60s, the earlier roughly +12 illustration depends on that score; it is not a certified character total. Precise Shot and Improved Precise Shot scale the operative ability contribution, not this separate trance bonus. Do not add it unconditionally to another Insight damage source such as Insightful Deadly. No Step Missed remains blocked by the antirequisite and unselected. Harper Agent.

The Mark: the first button of every fight.

  • Mark Target (Horizon Walker core, 4-second cooldown). You mark one enemy at a time. With all the Marking cores it has -10 AC, -25% fortification, -4 to all saves and -20% movement speed, and with the Horizon capstone it counts as a Favored Enemy, which switches on every “versus Favored Enemy” bonus in the build against it. The whole party benefits from the AC and fortification cut. Mark the thing that matters: the caster, the champion, the Reaper, the boss, not the nearest kobold. When the Marked target dies, re-Mark; the cooldown is 4 seconds so it is always ready.
  • Corner the Quarry (Horizon Walker tier 4, 20-second cooldown) is a shot at the Marked target for +40% damage that also roots it in place on a failed Reflex save against your Listen skill. On Reaper the root is 6 seconds (crowd control is never shorter than that) and monsters get +1.5 to saves per skull, so treat the root as a bonus, not a plan. Banish the Quarry adds 1d6 Force per level to the same shot. Press it whenever it is up on anything you have Marked.

The shots: what you press between the autoattacks.

  • Opening Shot (tier 1, 6 seconds). +30% damage and 3d6 Force; with Take the Opening, a target at full health takes another 1d10 Force per two levels, doubled if it is Marked. So the order on a new pull is Mark, then Opening Shot, before anything has touched the target.
  • Manyshot (Ranger 6 feat) and Scattershot (tier 4) share an 8-second cooldown. Manyshot fires three arrows at one target; Scattershot is a cone that hits everything in front of you. Both carry +2 critical threat range and multiplier, both trigger Powershot (1d10 Force per level on every critical, at 200% Ranged Power), and both trigger Two Places At Once: “When you use Manyshot or Scattershot, you gain Displacement for 12 seconds” (wiki). That last line is why you alternate them every 8 seconds even when you do not need the damage: it is how your 50% miss chance stays up for the whole fight.
  • Sniper Shot (Deepwood Stalker core, 6 seconds). +20% damage, +4 to hit, +2 threat range and multiplier, and the target is “vulnerable to sneak attack” for 4 seconds, so the sneak dice land even on something that is looking straight at you. Use it on the Marked target between the other shots.
  • Elemental Inferno (Arcane Archer tier 2, 3 seconds). A cone shot that leaves a stacking fire burn on everything it touches. It is cheap and quick; fire it into any clump, then go back to the cycle.
  • Hunt’s End (Shiradi tier 3, 9 seconds, from level 23). The source lists +250% damage and +20 threat range and describes an automatic critical. That wording does not independently establish bypass of all hit, confirmation and fortification checks, so realized critical damage remains conditional. Use it on the Marked target when available.
  • The epic strikes (from level 20) share one cooldown between them (Epic Destinies page: “all strikes share a cooldown”). The Pluck of a String (5 seconds) is the single-target one: +30% damage, +4 threat range, 2d10 Sonic per level, a stun on a critical, and it leaves a Chord of Fate on you, stacking to three. Arrow of Discord (15 seconds) is the cone that spends the Chords: +20% damage, +2 threat range, a stun of one second per Chord, and 10 spell points back at three. Shadowstrike (8 seconds) is the other cone: +30% damage, +2 multiplier, +3 threat range, Shadow Loss only if the struck opponent fails its Fortitude save (Shadowdancer page: “DC 20 + highest ability score + Assassinate Bonuses”), and with Dark Imbuement every hit you make for the next 10 seconds adds 1d6 per sneak die. Because they share a cooldown, the order matters: Pluck, Pluck, Pluck, then Discord on a boss; Shadowstrike first on a pack.

The bursts: windows you open on purpose.

  • Action Boost: Ranged Power (Deepwood tier 2, 30-second cooldown). +30 Ranged Power for 20 seconds. Open it when the Marked target is a boss or a champion and the epic strikes are about to come off cooldown.
  • Inexorable Advance (Shiradi tier 5 Epic Moment, 5-minute cooldown, from level 30). Thirty seconds of +30 Ranged Power, no Improved Precise Shot penalty, Cold and Light procs, and, the part that matters for survival, Archer’s Focus keeps building while you move. It is the one window in which you are allowed to run and shoot. Save it for the fight that cannot be fought from one spot.
  • Misty Step (Horizon Walker tier 5, 12 seconds) is both an escape and a burst: a dash with +5 Reflex and 20% incorporeality while you move, and Ranged Power equal to your character level for 12 seconds after any step or tumble. It is the button for the moment you have to move anyway.

The escapes: the buttons that keep you alive.

  • Misty Step, above. Twelve seconds is short; it is the first thing you press when something is in your face.
  • Walker’s Guidance (tier 4, 60 seconds): “You and targeted ally gain Evasion, Improved Evasion and +5 to Saving Throws vs. Traps for 12 seconds” (wiki). “I already have Evasion, so what is this for?” You do, from Ranger 9 on class lives and Rogue 2 on racial lives, and Walker’s Guidance adds three things on top of it. First, Improved Evasion: Evasion only helps when you pass the Reflex save (no damage instead of half); Improved Evasion covers the failed save (half damage instead of full), and until Shadowdancer’s core upgrades you at 35 in the destiny order this button is the only Improved Evasion you have, so a natural 1 against a big trap or a breath weapon is a full hit without it. Second, +5 to saves against traps, which turns a borderline roll into a pass, and a pass with Evasion is zero. Third, Evasion for an ally: the melee in front of you, the healer, anyone about to eat a breath; a heavy-armor friend cannot evade (the wiki notes they “effectively only receive +5 Saving Throws vs. Traps”), but a light-armor one gets the whole shield. This Guide buys Walker’s Guidance at character level 14; it is not available earlier in the selected purchase order. The moments to press it: before a trap corridor, when a dragon or caster winds up, or on an ally who is about to be hit.
  • Dimension Door (Horizon Walker core, once per rest, 10-minute cooldown). Teleports you to the quest entrance. It is a full reset, not a dodge: use it when the fight is lost, you are the last one up, or the death timer is running on everyone else and your job is to not join them.
  • Tumble (the skill). Tumbling gives you the Misty Step Ranged Power without the dash, and the Skills section explains the third charge. It is also how you cross a Fear Reaper’s line of sight in one move.

The rotationbuild decision

The wiki publishes abilities, not rotations. This one is derived from the cooldowns above and from one rule: Displacement from Two Places At Once lasts 12 seconds and Manyshot and Scattershot share an 8-second cooldown, so alternating them keeps the miss chance up with 4 seconds to spare.

Before the quest. Precision, Archer’s Focus and the Shiradi mantle lit. Flaming Arrows and Morphic Arrows switched on (20 spell points each; they stay on). Haste and Know the Angles cast; both last two minutes, and you recast them when the icons drop, not mid-fight. Improved Precise Shot stance off unless the first room is a corridor.

A pack. Pick your spot first (see standing still below). Mark the most dangerous thing in the group. Opening Shot on something at full health. Scattershot into the group: this is also what starts Displacement. Elemental Inferno on the clump. Shadowstrike. Then normal fire while Dark Imbuement runs, and from then on Manyshot and Scattershot alternately every 8 seconds, so Displacement never lapses and Powershot keeps proccing on the criticals. Re-Mark as the Marked target dies. If the pack breaks toward you, read the next part before anything else.

A boss. Improved Precise Shot stance off. Mark. Corner the Quarry on cooldown. Pluck of a String on cooldown; after three Plucks have built three Chords, Arrow of Discord. Hunt’s End and Sniper Shot are on their own timers and go between the strikes. Manyshot every 8 seconds for Displacement and Powershot. Open Action Boost: Ranged Power when the strikes are about to line up, and Inexorable Advance if the boss is one you have to kite.

Helpless targets. Anything Nerve Venom paralyses, or anything the party has held, is helpless: it takes 50% more damage from every source (Helpless page) and Cruelty of the Hunt adds 10% more on top. Switch to it.

Standing still, and where. Archer’s Focus does not start until you have been stationary for 3 seconds, adds a stack per hit to 25, and loses one stack per 3 seconds while you are moving or not shooting. Hunter’s Focus gives you 1d4+4 stacks back each time you have stood still for 6 seconds; the wiki’s own note says that because stacks fall off one every 3 seconds and this gives at least five every 6, “it’s possible to stutter-step with this enhancement and remain at near-maximum stacks, while still retaining mobility”. So the rule is not “never move”; it is “move once and far, then stop for six seconds”. Drifting left and right is the worst thing you can do: every second of it is a stack gone and a -4 to hit (the moving penalty Shot on the Run would remove). Jumping in place is not moving: “jumping while otherwise stationary is not considered moving for the purposes of accumulating stacks” (Archer’s Focus page), so against archers and casters jump straight up and keep shooting at the apex. Before a pull, pick a spot with a long sightline and something solid behind you, shoot from there, and let the Marked target and the party come to you; Feywild Attunement keeps you “always in Point Blank range”, so distance costs you nothing and costs the enemy the walk.

Your defences, layer by layer (wiki)

Damage reaches you through a series of checks, and the build has something at each one. Knowing which layer is doing the work tells you which one to lean on when a fight goes wrong.

Layer 1: not being the target. Monsters attack whoever is at the top of their hate list, and hate is “the calculation of how much damage you’ve dealt” (Threat page). A bow that does the most damage in the group will be the target unless it generates less hate per point. The build cuts ranged threat three ways: Queen’s Diplomacy -30%, Cover of Darkness -30%, Pianissimo -25% (-35% at rank 3), and the endgame kit adds Distant Diversion. Those are all epic; before level 20 there is nothing, which is one reason levelling is harder than cap. The practical side: the first few seconds of a fight decide who the pack chases. Let the melee hit it first, or Mark and let the Mark’s debuffs do their work for a moment before you open up. If you take aggro anyway, the pack is coming to you and you are moving, so Archer’s Focus is gone too; it is a double loss, which is why threat is the first layer and not the last.

Layer 2: the attack misses. Three separate rolls can turn an attack away, each checked on its own (Dodge bonus page): Dodge, Concealment, and Incorporeality.

  • Dodge is a flat chance to avoid a physical attack, and it is the one that Reaper does not weaken. Passive dodge is Planar Dodge 3 plus Combat Archery 2, plus one dodge item (item dodge does not stack: only the best applies), plus Reaper’s Evasion from the Reaper tree. The cap is 25% plus Dauntless Marking’s +3, limited by the armor’s Max Dex Bonus; Lithe in Shadowdancer raises that. Reaper 10 section has the exact numbers for the kit. While levelling, dodge is small (3 to 5% from enhancements until a dodge item shows up at 5), so do not count on it.
  • Concealment is Blur (20%) or Displacement (50%), and Two Places At Once gives you Displacement for 12 seconds every time you fire Manyshot or Scattershot. On Reaper it is bypassed by 5% plus 5% per skull, capped at 30% (Reaper difficulty page), so Displacement is 50% on Elite, about 40% at R1 and 20% at R10. Still worth keeping up every fight, because it costs nothing.
  • Incorporeality (Ghostly on an item, Misty Step’s 20% while stepping) is a separate miss chance again, bypassed up to 35% on Reaper.
  • Armor Class is the fourth miss roll and the build mostly ignores it. The Mark is what AC-based defence this build has, and it is on the enemy: -10 AC on the Marked target is for the party’s benefit, not yours.

Layer 3: the hit does less. Physical Resistance Rating reduces physical damage by 100/(100+PRR) (PRR page): 20 PRR takes a sixth off, 50 takes a third, 100 takes half. Each point is about +1% effective hit points. Light armor gives PRR equal to your Base Attack Bonus with proficiency, which Ranger has; the rest is items. Magical Resistance Rating does the same for fire, cold, electric, acid, sonic, force, light, negative and the rest (MRR page), capped at 100 in light armor. Survivalist adds +3 PRR; the Divine epic past lives add up to +36; the Reaper tree’s Grim Barricade adds more. The fully filled level-36 kit contributes unverified gear PRR (modeled raw 133); the whole-character defense totals remain unverified. At level 10 you have perhaps 20 PRR, which cuts physical damage by a sixth. That gap is why levelling hurts.

Layer 4: the hit is not a critical or a sneak attack. Fortification is a percentage chance to turn a critical hit or sneak attack against you into a normal hit (Fortification page); “most players strive for roughly +10% fortification per level”. Dauntless Marking does nothing for you here: it is the enemy’s fortification the Mark cuts. Your own comes from items. Selected level-36 gear Fortification: 226% for the selected kit; see the current source audit. While levelling, the Lotus Mail (59%) plus the Nocturne Ring (Insightful 29%) at 5-9 is the first real fortification; before that you have none, and every critical hit lands.

Layer 5: the save. Three saves (Saving throw page), and this build is uneven across them.

  • Reflex is the strong one: Ranger and Rogue both have good Reflex progressions (Ranger +6 at level 9; class table), Insightful Reflexes puts your Intelligence modifier on it instead of Dexterity, and Evasion from Ranger 9 (or Rogue 2) turns a passed save into no damage at all. Shadowdancer’s The Darkest Luck core upgrades it to Improved Evasion at 35 in the destiny order (a failed save is half damage). Walker’s Guidance gives both for 12 seconds on demand. Traps, breath weapons, fireballs, lightning bolts: this is where the build is safe, as long as it is in light armor and not helpless.
  • Fortitude is middling: Ranger’s Fortitude progression is good (+6 at 9), but the Constitution modifier behind it is small. Poison, disease, stuns from melee specials, and the Doom Reaper’s chain are Fortitude effects.
  • Will remains a concern. Starting Wisdom 8 has a −1 modifier before other sources, not a permanently equipped −1 modifier. The selected Wisdom gear can improve the Wisdom contribution; calculate the final modifier after same-type overlaps, then combine applicable class progressions, gear and buffs. Ranger’s listed +3 at level 9 and Rogue’s +0 at level 1 are only part of a multiclass character’s save, not its final total. The current gear subtotals and final Will remain unverified. Hold Person, Command, fear, dance and the other mind-affecting effects all test Will. A held archer is helpless: it cannot act normally, takes extra damage and loses Evasion (Helpless; Evasion). Improved Wisdom is not immunity to those effects.

Layer 6: hit points. Ranger d10, Rogue d8, a modest Constitution, False Life and Vitality from items, and the Reaper tree’s bonus HP (capped by level: 50 at levels 1-5, 100 at 6-10, 200 at 11-15, 400 at 16-20; Reaper enhancements page). At cap the kit adds about 108 from augments and the Titan’s Blood feat adds 10%. There is no large HP pool here; it is the last layer, and the point of the first five is that it is rarely reached.

Layer 7: healing. Not yours. In-combat self-healing on Reaper is (11 - skulls)/25 of normal: 40% at R1, 24% at R5, 4% at R10 (Reaper difficulty page). Potions and the Healing Spring ability are for Elite and the first few skulls only. What works at every skull is someone else healing you: outgoing healing is only cut to (26 - skulls)/25, 64% at R10. So the real healing plan is Use Magic Device for scrolls on other people and a party healer for you, and while levelling, a cleric hireling (the Hireling page: they can be summoned in any quest except Solo difficulty and raids, and Gold Seal contracts can be used two levels early). Shiradi’s Fey Favor core also gives you +15 HP and +25 spell points per core; that is where your Dimension Door and imbue points come from, not healing.

What kills this character, and what to do (wiki)

Ranked by how often it happens, with the mechanic from the wiki and the response. The responses are build decisions.

1. A melee pack reaches you. This is the common death, and it is the one the whole build is designed to prevent. When a pack is on you, three things are true at once: you are the target (layer 1 failed), you are moving so Archer’s Focus is draining, and you are taking hits through a small PRR pool. Response, in order: Misty Step away, far, in one go, through a doorway if there is one; stop and shoot from the new spot for six seconds so Hunter’s Focus refills the stacks; fire Scattershot into the pack as it comes so Displacement is up; Walker’s Guidance if anything in the pack breathes or casts. Do not run in circles. If the pack is faster than you (Marked targets are -20% speed, the others are not), Dimension Door is the reset. Before the next pull, change what you did: let the melee engage first, Mark before you shoot, and pick the spot with the long sightline so the pack has to walk.

2. Monster Champions. Champions are the crowned enemies on Hard, Elite and Reaper: 9% of normal monsters and 66% of orange-named mini-bosses on Elite and Reaper, in three tiers, and the red-crowned tier 3 “is only found in Reaper difficulty” (Monster Champion page). All of them hit harder. The ones that hurt this build in particular: Dodge Bypass (Acheron, Beast Mark, Mark of Law tiers 2-3: “attacks ignore Dodge”), Fortification Bypass (Acheron, Beast Mark, Infernal Pact), Vulnerable on Hit (Infernal Pact, Pandemonium: stacking extra damage taken), the elemental DoTs (Flame Born, Soul of Ice, Skyborn, Soul of Cruelty: stacking damage over time that keeps ticking after you have left), Healing Debuff on Hit (Pandemonium, Shadow Marked), and True Sight, which 15 of the 20 tier-3 champions have and which makes Invisibility useless. Response: a champion is what you Mark. Read its title (the examine window names the variety) and if it is a DoT or Vulnerable type, it is also the thing you do not let touch you even once. Deflect Arrows on Skyborn champions is a reason to let the melee take that one.

3. Reapers. They “spawn at the last location of the associated enemy” with a five-second delay, every one is an Incorporeal evil outsider immune to poison and to ground effects, and the regular ones sense you through walls at 18 metres (Reaper race page). Two things about them matter more than any other for this build.

  • They are incorporeal, and your arrows miss half the time unless they have Ghost Touch. “An incorporeal creature’s 50% chance to avoid damage does not apply to attacks with ghost touch weapons or ammunition” (Ghost Touch page). The build gets Ghost Touch at level 23 from Shiradi’s Vision core and from the Reaper tree’s Memento: Reaper’s Imbuement (level 21). Before 23 it has none unless the bow or the arrows carry it. That is a levelling-set gap of the first order and it goes in the gear rework; until then, Ghost Touch arrows or a Ghost Touch bow for any Reaper quest, or you are shooting half as hard at the most dangerous thing in the room.
  • Each type wants something different from you. The Carnage Reaper is pure melee, stays in melee range, and every hit stacks 20% Vulnerability on you, ten times. It is the one that kills archers: the response is distance, Mark, and the party; never let it stack. The Famine Reaper is a caster that “teleports to its target if it loses line of sight for 8 seconds”, so hiding from it only brings it to you; stay in sight at range and kill it, and never stand next to it (four seconds in its melee range applies Slowing Touch, which slows you and stops you tumbling). The Fear Reaper stacks a damage debuff on anyone it can see within range and “isn’t applied within a small radius around the Reaper”, so the two safe places are out of its sight or right on top of it; it also tries to hold its position, so breaking line of sight works on this one. The Plague Reaper puts a strong healing debuff on you and closes to melee; it is the one to kill first when the party has a healer, because the healer is what keeps you alive. At five skulls and up the Vengeance Reaper drops blue circles that explode for heavy Evil damage (step out of them; Evasion does not cover it if the damage is not a Reflex effect) and buffs its allies when it dies, and the Despair Reaper buffs enemy saves in an aura. At seven and up the Doom Reaper is a red-named melee immune to crowd control that stacks Marks of Doom on its target and, at seven stacks, lands “massive bonus damage”: if it marks you, you leave, by Misty Step then Dimension Door if it follows.

4. A failed Will save. Evaluate the actual life’s complete class progressions, total equipped Wisdom modifier, and applicable typed gear/buff bonuses; do not reuse a fixed −1 Wisdom modifier from the starting allocation. The new loadout does not certify the final Will save or eliminate failed-save risk. While held you are helpless (see layer 5), Evasion is off and you cannot press Misty Step. Build decision: retain Will protection and party support, and check whether a particular removal or immunity applies to the effect you face. The Mark’s −4 to enemy saves does not protect your own save. This correction adds no new augment or selected ability.

5. Knockdown. Dogs, wolves (including the mechanical ones), air elementals and minotaurs knock you prone (Knockdown page), and so do Command, Cometfall, Earthquake and Grease. Prone, you are not shooting, you are not moving, and the pack catches up. “Once down, Balance can help you get up faster” (Balance page), which is why Balance takes the spare skill points. Protection from Evil blocks Command, and the Horizon Walker Protection from Evil and Good rank is in the build.

6. Elemental damage. Fire, cold, electric and acid go through Magical Resistance Rating, not PRR, and MRR is capped at 100 in light armor. Breath weapons and most area spells allow a Reflex save, so Evasion handles those. What it does not handle is the damage with no save: the champion DoTs above, Vengeance Reaper circles, lava, Fire Shield on something you are standing in. Response: absorption items for an elemental quest (the Greaves of Seasons’ Warding swap at cap), the Reaper tree’s Memento: Reaper’s Steadfastness (+30 energy resistance and +100 fortification for 30 seconds), and position.

7. Enemy archers and casters. They do not have to reach you, so distance is not a defence. Response: jump in place while you shoot (Archer’s Focus keeps building), Displacement up, Mark the caster first (its -4 to saves is for the party’s crowd control; its -25% fortification is for your criticals), and kill the caster before the melee. A caster in a pack is the Marked target by default.

8. Dungeon Alert. Pulling too much, too fast, raises the alert; at red it is “as high as +28 to saves” and extra fortification for every enemy in the dungeon, and at purple monsters break out of crowd control (Dungeon Alert page). An archer kiting a crowd down a corridor is the textbook way to raise it. Response: kill what you pull, pull what you can kill, and do not drag a pack through a door into the next room.

9. Traps. This is the one place the build is safe by design: Evasion with Intelligence on Reflex, Walker’s Guidance for +5 against traps, and Rogue 1 for Search and Disable. The Spell wards in the Underdark quests are Will saves and cannot be evaded; walk around those.

The survival routine

Five situations, each with the exact sequence. Practise them on Elite until they are reflex; on Reaper there is no time to think.

  • Something is in melee range. Misty Step away (the dash itself has 20% incorporeality and +5 Reflex), stop, six seconds, shoot. If it is still on you, Scattershot for Displacement, then Walker’s Guidance, then Dimension Door.
  • A Reaper spawns. Name it. Carnage or Doom: distance, now, and Mark it so the party can kill it. Famine: stay in its sight at range and Mark it. Fear: break line of sight or close in; do not stand in the middle distance. Plague: Mark it and kill it first. Vengeance: watch the ground.
  • You are held or dancing. Nothing you press works. Block if the hold allows it (blocking reduces damage while helpless: Helpless page). Say so in party chat; the counter is theirs.
  • You are under half health. On Elite or R1-R3, a potion is still worth drinking (40% to 32% of its value). On R4 and up it is not; the answer is to stop taking damage (Misty Step, distance, Displacement) and let the healer work, because outgoing healing is still 64% at R10.
  • The healer is down. The death timer is 6 seconds per skull (a minute at R10). You are now the party’s least hittable member. Kite nothing; find the spot with the sightline, keep Displacement up, kill the thing that killed the healer, and if it cannot be done, Dimension Door and let the timer run out on everyone before you try again.

Where the levelling set is thin. The set was lifted slot for slot from a repeater dataset and carries that build’s priorities, and the gaps are visible in its own lines (Gear section): at 1-4 the armor’s PRR 7 and the bracer’s Sheltering 7 are the same bonus type, so the total is 7, and there is no fortification at all; at 5-9 the only Constitution item is dropped for Seeker boots and the cloak upgrades at 7 and 9 both lose False Life; at 15-19 one bracer slot is spent on healing spell power the build cannot use; at 20-29 the necklace and bracers carry Strength, Shatter, Wisdom and Wizardry, nothing for a bow, while fortification sits at 94% and False Life is gone.   6. Let the melee open. With no threat reduction, the first arrow decides who the pack follows. Mark, wait for the first swing, then shoot.
  7. Potions still work here. At R1-R3 a Cure Serious potion is a third of its value, which is still a third. Keep a stack and drink at half health, not at a quarter.

Rules the build depends on

The wiki rules the guide leans on, in one place. Elsewhere they are referenced, not repeated.

  • Trance antirequisite: Know the Angles and No Step Missed each read “can not be trained if you have any other Trance ability.” Know the Angles is the one taken.
  • Imbue toggle: Arcane Archer’s selected Flaming supplies the damage imbue. At 30, its base die plus Scion and alignment-conditional Harbinger give 4d6 while Chaotic / 2d6 otherwise. The selected Emerald adds one die when its ML34 boots are equipped, for 5d6 / 3d6; the complete outfit requires 36. Only one damage imbue is active at a time; Morphic Arrows can run alongside it. Selected Crooked Touch is Whirling Wrists, so no dice from the unselected Flickering Fingers alternative are added. Applicable sources and conditions.
  • One mantle, one strike timer: only one destiny mantle is active (Shiradi’s Prism), and all epic strikes share a cooldown; both rules decide the third-tree choice.
  • Bonus types: same-type bonuses normally use the highest applicable value; a named exception needs its own evidence. Current overlaps include Reflection’s Enhancement ranged speed with Haste, duplicate Greater Marksmanship, the Celestial Sapphire Ring’s Profane Well Rounded with higher matching Lunar ability bonuses, and Globe’s Exceptional Intelligence with Calamitous’s Dimlight. Know the Angles and another Insight damage source do not simply add. The current component audit distinguishes same-type suppression from independently supported mixed-type combinations; the former Pendant, Litany and Monolith sources belong to the archived kits.
  • Keen and Improved Critical: Keen “does not stack with Improved Critical feat” (Keen page); this limits Keen’s contribution, not the useful life of the whole bow. Sapphire Sting has listed 19-20/x3; Keen is not listed. Its post-Improved-Critical interaction is not inferred from Bow of Sinew’s item-specific explanation.
  • Ranged Alacrity and Haste: the retained same-type rule uses the higher applicable Enhancement speed bonus, not their sum (Ranged Alacrity; Haste). The historical Haste-always-up model compares 20% with an assumed 15% baseline: 1.20/1.15 ≈ 1.0435, about +4.35%.   • Material bypass: since U81.4 unbroken DR costs 25% of weapon damage; a bow that counts as the metal (Thunder-Forged, Melancholic Flames or Chill) makes the matching Pierce feat redundant.
  • Optimizer scope: the current Wisdom priority table is generated in the exact saved query order, with caps, best-effort floors and item pins shown separately. The optimizer is not a complete weapon/critical/proc/rotation DPS simulation, and the preserved historical bow model is not a validation of the new kit.
  • Optimizer and exclusions: ranked positive affixes do not certify negative effects, ownership or procurement. The saved query records its pins and exclusions, including the no-event/limited-time policy with Festive ability diamonds as the only exception. A source-screen pass is not proof of every exact drop or vendor route.
  • Lexicographic order is not free: the preserved higher objective is subject to its stated cap; an above-cap raw amount can change. Best-effort floors must be checked against the actual result. The practical Wisdom recipe explicitly trades two modeled CON for Good Luck’s save/skill bonuses after the solve; it is not a second optimal solve.
